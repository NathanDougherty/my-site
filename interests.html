Projects at the Intersection of Code and Markets

I learn best by building things. Most of what I know about markets and software comes from projects where I had to make an idea actually work, not just understand it on paper. Each project pushes me to connect code, data, and finance in a new way.

One of my favorite projects is a clone of the Robinhood trading app, built in TypeScript. Recreating a brokerage interface taught me how much engineering goes into something that looks simple: live price updates, order flows, portfolio views, and a clean user experience that people trust with their money.

Another project, biotech-options-signals, is a Python tool that looks for trading signals in options on biotech stocks. Biotech is a fun area because big events, like drug trial results and FDA decisions, can move prices sharply. Working on it taught me how to clean messy market data, test ideas carefully, and avoid fooling myself with results that only look good by chance.

These projects, along with my internships in quantitative research and trading operations, keep pulling me toward the same goal: building tools that help people make better decisions with data. Visualization is a big part of that, because a clear chart can explain risk faster than a table of numbers.
