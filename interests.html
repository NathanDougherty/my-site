Projects at the Intersection of Code and Markets

I learn best by building things. Most of what I know about markets and software comes from projects where I had to make an idea actually work, not just understand it on paper. Two projects I'm especially proud of sit right where systems engineering meets quantitative finance.

The first is a high-performance, in-memory vector database written in C++20. It supports both exact and approximate nearest-neighbor search using two well-known indexing methods, HNSW and LSH. To make it fast, I wrote SIMD-optimized distance calculations for both ARM64 Apple Silicon and x86-64 processors, so similarity searches take full advantage of modern CPUs. It taught me how much performance depends on memory layout and low-level details.

The second is a research project on leveraged ETFs, built in Python with pandas and the Alpaca API. I backtested systematic trading strategies on 2x and 3x leveraged ETFs using more than six years of historical market data, then compared how each strategy held up in different market conditions using returns, drawdowns, and volatility. Leveraged ETFs are tricky because daily rebalancing can quietly eat away at returns, so measuring risk carefully mattered as much as finding gains.

My internships pushed these skills further. At Flow Traders I built low-latency trading infrastructure and observability dashboards, and at RA Capital Management I built automated research workflows that turned hours of work into about 100 seconds.
